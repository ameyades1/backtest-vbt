# Scale exposure gradually, and let winners breathe

The evidence does not support adding another on/off regime switch to S44984096 to fix its 2017–2019 and 2025 lag. India VIX works poorly as a risk-off trigger. A "% of NIFTY 500 above 200 DMA" filter has no peer-reviewed out-of-sample support anywhere, India included. Index trend filters, which S44984096 already uses for entries (NIFTY 500 above its 200-day SMA), mostly cut drawdown in long bear markets and lose a steady trickle of money in sideways ones. The strongest regime evidence, in both US and Indian samples, is for **scaling exposure gradually by the strategy's own realised volatility, or by its own equity curve**. Published results roughly double momentum's Sharpe ratio, but they are long-short, mostly in-sample and lightly costed. On exits, studies agree on one pattern: **wide or slow exits (time exits, lowest-low channels, 3–4+ ATR chandeliers, slow EMAs) survive costs and holdout periods, and tight give-back trailing stops do not.** No study compares chandelier, ATR, EMA and time exits on the same entry with net costs, so this repo has to run that test itself. Be clear about what this means for 2017–2019 and 2025. Those were rotation and midcap-bear years, and no filter in the literature reliably fixes them. A realistic aim is a shallower drawdown and less time under water, not a higher CAGR. This is research, not investment advice.

## Momentum's weak spots are real, but binary filters treat them poorly

The academic case that momentum depends on the regime is solid. Cooper, Gutierrez and Hameed define the market state as the lagged 3-year market return. They find that **after a negative 3-year market return the momentum premium is roughly zero**, and nearly all momentum profit comes in up states ([Typeset](https://typeset.io/pdf/market-states-and-momentum-2o1ubo5j2l.pdf)). Daniel and Moskowitz show that crashes cluster in "panic" states, when a bear market ends and losers rebound sharply. In bear markets the momentum portfolio's up-market beta is -1.51 against a down-market beta of -0.70. That asymmetry comes from the **short losers leg** ([NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)). For a long-only book like S44984096, the practical problem is therefore weak expected returns in down and sideways states, not crash risk.

The fix with the strongest support is volatility scaling. Barroso and Santa-Clara scale momentum by its own trailing realised volatility. That **raised the Sharpe ratio from 0.53 to 0.97**. Over the 2000s, plain momentum lost 28% of wealth while the managed version gained 88% ([CFA Institute](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/blog/2018/01/17/timing-the-market-momentum-and-beyond)). Daniel and Moskowitz's dynamic version "approximately doubles the alpha and Sharpe ratio" across markets ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/journal-article/momentum-crashes)). There is a caveat. Generic volatility timing of a portfolio (Moreira-Muir style) beat the unmanaged version in only **53 of 103 equity portfolios out of sample**, and reasonable OOS versions earned lower Sharpe ratios ([Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). Scaling momentum by its own risk holds up better than timing volatility in general, but neither result comes free of costs.

Simple index trend filters look much weaker once data-mining and costs are handled honestly. Zakamulin uses 155 years of data with rolling windows and finds "no statistically significant evidence" that MA timing beat the market in the second half of the sample. The value came from bear states ([SSRN 2677212](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2677212)). He also showed that a strong published MA result came from look-ahead bias. Corrected, it was "only marginally better" than buy-and-hold ([RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:irvfin:v:18:y:2018:i:2:p:317-327)). A data-snooping-robust test of 6,406 rules across 41 markets found results "very sensitive to the introduction of moderate transaction costs" ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11408-023-00433-2.pdf)). In sideways tape, the S&P 500 crossed its 200 DMA **165 times since 2000**. One 25-year test had 20 winners averaging +13.7% and 70 whipsaws averaging -1.3% ([StockCharts](https://stockcharts.com/articles/arthurhill/2019/02/just-how-effective-is-the-200-day-moving-average.html)). A ±3% band cut signals from 27 to 4 ([StockCharts 2022](https://d.stockcharts.com/articles/chartwatchers/2022/02/reduce-whipsaws-and-improve-pe-590.html)). A break below a 200 DMA that is still rising was "almost always a whipsaw" ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2026/03/23/200-dma-broke-investor-should-know)). Expect any filter picked in-sample to lose a good part of its edge. McLean and Pontiff find predictor returns **26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication** ([PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)).

Breadth filters are the weakest of the three. I found no academic out-of-sample test of "% of stocks above 200 DMA" or the advance-decline line as a timing filter. The practitioner event studies mostly point the other way. Readings below 40% were followed by consistent 2–3 month rebounds ([SentimenTrader](https://www.sentimentrader.com/blog/recovering-from-under-the-200-day-average)), and readings of 92% or more were followed by favourable returns ([SentimenTrader/BofA](https://sentimentrader.com/blog/why-strong-might-be-too-strong)). A rule that goes risk-off on low breadth would sit out exactly those rebound months. One more trap for this repo: a breadth series built from today's NIFTY 500 list has survivorship bias. It must be computed from the point-in-time constituent file the backtest already uses.

## Indian evidence: real momentum premium, thin evidence on filters

Momentum is large and well documented in India. The IIMA four-factor data (October 1993 to December 2013, survivorship-corrected, illiquid stocks excluded) show the WML factor averaging **about 21.9% a year** ([IIMA](https://www.iima.ac.in/publication/four-factor-model-indian-equities-market)). On 450 BSE stocks, Singh and Walia find that risk-managed momentum in the Barroso-Santa-Clara style **roughly doubles the adjusted Sharpe ratio and significantly improves downside risk**. The test is in-sample and without Indian costs ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/fbbsrw/v11y2022i3p347-358.html)). An Emerald study finds the same for time-series momentum ([Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJOEM-09-2023-1518/full/html)). So India confirms the global pattern: own-volatility scaling has the best support, and nobody has tested it out of sample net of STT.

India VIX is a poor switch. NIFTY returns are negatively and asymmetrically linked to *same-period* VIX changes ([NSE WP9](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/research/content/res_WorkingPaper9.pdf)). As a *predictor*, though, a VIX-plus-RSI composite from 2011 to 2025 is **contrarian**: high-VIX, overbought regimes tend to be followed by reversals ([F1000Research](https://f1000research.com/articles/15-427/v1)). RBI commentary notes that VIX above 20 does not reliably mean poor near-term returns ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=21337)). Spikes in VIX come with or after the fall, so a VIX exit de-risks late and re-enters late. The US VIX term-structure evidence points the same way: it works as a contrarian signal, and its edge was concentrated in 2008 ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/volatility-effects/timing-and-hedging-the-roll-return-for-vix-futures/)). At most, VIX could be one input to position size.

The Indian index record shows why S44984096 is struggling now. Nifty200 Momentum 30 fell **31.8% from 27 September 2024 to 7 April 2025**, and on 5 November 2026 it was still 21.6% below that peak. Yet it has had no losing 3- or 5-year rolling window ([Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/blog/momentum-strategies-underperforming-2025-data-insights/)). In a recent six-month correction, the momentum index fell 27% against 12% for NIFTY 50 ([Curious Investing](https://curiousinvestinginsights.substack.com/p/momentum-or-mayhem-unpacking-the)). The episode looks like high-beta midcap rotation, not a broad crash. An index-level filter on NIFTY 500 would have reacted slowly, because the large-cap index held up better than the momentum names did.

The few Indian overlay tests are cautionary. Freefincal tested a 6-month over 12-month MA switch on Midcap150 Momentum 50. It flipped only 2–3 times in 5–7 years, yet generally produced **higher max drawdown, higher volatility and longer time under water** than plain rebalancing. It failed in the fast March 2020 crash, and taxes were excluded ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/?p=93180)). Momo India's "cash when the strategy NAV is below its 200-DMA" test (2007–2024) is paywalled ([Momo India](https://momoindia.substack.com/p/backtest-reducing-momentum-drawdowns)). StockViz says plainly that "the jury is still out" on crash avoidance in Indian momentum ([StockViz](https://stockviz.substack.com/p/momentum-without-the-crash)). Costs matter more in India than in the US papers. In a net-of-tax Freefincal study, **high-turnover momentum made 8.51% CAGR against 19.43% for a liquidity-scaled, low-turnover version** ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-indian-momentum-investing-just-a-liquidity-illusion/)). Every forced round trip in this repo costs roughly 0.86% in fees (`rules.FEE`, Sharekhan 0.30% brokerage) plus 0.2% slippage. It can also turn a long-term gain into 20% STCG. Whipsaw-prone filters are therefore more expensive here than in the US literature.

## Exits: wide and slow ones survive, tight trailing stops do not

The theory has a clear answer. Kaminski and Lo prove that under a random walk a stop-loss always lowers expected return. A stop adds value only when returns are serially correlated ([MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)). Lo and Remorov test US stocks from 1964 to 2014. **Tight stops underperformed buy-and-hold after costs.** Wide 10-day stops matched buy-and-hold on a risk-adjusted basis while cutting max drawdown. **Executing one day late beat immediate execution**, because prices partly reverted. Stock autocorrelation also flipped sign between the two halves of the sample, so a stop tuned in one era can fail in the next ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth)). Han, Zhou and Zhu is the headline "stops tame momentum" paper. A 15% stop lifted Sharpe from 0.17 to 0.40 and cut the worst month from -49.8% to -17.4%. But the result is **gross of costs and driven mostly by the short leg** ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/stop-losses-to-avoid-stock-momentum-crashes/)). Lempérière et al. find long trends intact over two centuries, while **shorter trends have "significantly withered"** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.3274)). A fast trailing exit is in effect a short-trend signal.

The practitioner head-to-heads point the same way. The comparison below uses the only reproducible numbers in the notes.

| Exit family | Evidence | Net result | Robustness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time exit (fixed holding period) | setup4alpha, Russell 1000, 105,708 trades, after commission and slippage | **+0.52%/trade; 12-month hold +1.03%** | Best of the visible exits; in-sample, per trade |
| Lowest-low channel | same | +0.24%/trade, about 0.1% better than close-only channels at every lookback | Consistent across lookbacks |
| Close-only channel | same | +0.17% (5-day version -0.17%) | Tightest setting fails |
| Give-back trailing stop (% of open profit) | same | **-0.02% to -0.14%/trade** (tightest t = -3.6) | Worst family |
| Chandelier 22/4×ATR on an EMA-cross system | StockCharts 1997–2017, point-in-time constituents, no slippage | CAR **below 7%**, against 8.46% for the plain 50/200 cross | Hurt return; drawdown similar |
| Trailing stop plus profit target | same | Worst combination tested | — |
| Wide % stop on Nifty 500 momentum | themarketshack, 5-year holdout | 20% stop: Calmar 0.382 vs 0.262 with no stop; 15% stop cost 4.3 pts CAGR | Non-monotonic; costs only 0.03%/side; unverified |

Sources: [setup4alpha](https://setup4alpha.substack.com/p/trailing-stop-vs-fixed-stop-tested), [StockCharts](https://articles.stockcharts.com/article/articles-arthurhill-2017-05-systemtrader---trailing-stops-versus-profit-targets-for-a-moving-average-crossover-system), [themarketshack](https://themarketshack.com/projects/we-ran-400-momentum-tests-on-nifty-500-stocks-across-20-years-one-parameter-matters-more-than-everything-else).

The chandelier exit is defined as highest high over 22 bars minus 3×ATR. In its raw form it can *fall* when ATR widens, so a correct implementation ratchets it so it never moves down ([IncredibleCharts](https://www.incrediblecharts.com/indicators/chandelier_exits.php)). At 3–4 ATR it is a sensible design, but no study shows it beating a slow trend exit after costs. Carver's volatility-based trailing stop of about 4× daily volatility implies roughly a 6.5-week holding period ([the7circles](https://the7circles.uk/?p=24323)). That is still faster than S44984096's weekly Supertrend exit. The overall result: **time exits and slow, wide trend exits hold up across studies. Tight ATR, chandelier and give-back stops look good mainly in gross or in-sample tests.** The one Indian holdout test shows a sweet spot around 20% rather than "tighter is better". It used unrealistically low costs, so it flatters tight stops.

S44984096 is already built the way this evidence favours. Its 0.25R test after 25 days is a conditional time exit, and its weekly Supertrend turn is a slow trend exit. Its history (about 23% winners, a 29-loss streak, the top 10 trades earning more than all net profit) is typical of a profile that a tighter trailing stop would damage most, because cutting the right tail of a few big winners is exactly what tight stops do. The open question is not whether to trail tighter. It is whether a wide chandelier or a slower EMA exit can match the Supertrend on CAGR while giving back less in sideways years.

## Prioritised backtest variants for S44984096

Every variant below should run through `release/s44984096/backtest.py` on point-in-time NIFTY 500 from July 2006. Keep the existing costs: `rules.FEE` of brokerage, STT, stamp, exchange, SEBI and GST, plus 0.1% slippage per side. Exits should be close-confirmed and filled at the **next open, not at the stop level**, which matches Lo and Remorov's finding that next-day execution is fine and captures gap and circuit risk honestly. Add three robustness checks to each run: (a) slippage doubled to 0.2% per side, (b) STCG/LTCG tax drag reported as a separate column, and (c) the number of extra round trips the change creates. Use an anchored walk-forward: fit 2006–2012 and test 2013–2016, then extend in 4-year steps, ending with a 2021–2026 holdout. Report full-period results, 2017–2019, 2020, and September 2024 to date separately. Judge on max drawdown, longest time under water, worst losing streak and Calmar ratio before CAGR. Accept a change only if a **plateau** of neighbouring parameters improves Calmar without cutting CAGR by more than about 1.5 points. Then rerun the random-pick test (median currently 16.4%) to confirm the edge does not come from which stocks happened to be picked.

| Priority | Variant | Grid | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Equity-curve volatility scaling** of `RISK`: risk = 1% × min(1, target vol / 60-day realised vol of the strategy's equity curve), checked weekly | target 12/15/18% annualised; lookback 40/60/120 days | Strongest academic support (Barroso-Santa-Clara, Daniel-Moskowitz, Singh-Walia India). Changes sizing only, not round trips, so costs stay low. Builds on the existing 10%/15% drawdown rules |
| 2 | **Graded market state instead of a binary filter**: full risk when NIFTY 500 is above its 200-SMA *and* the SMA is rising; half risk when only one holds; no new entries when neither holds. Optionally add a ±2–3% band | SMA 150/200/250; band 0/2/3% | Addresses whipsaw and "rising-SMA breaks are whipsaws". Halving size instead of switching off reduces missed rebounds |
| 3 | **Cooper-style long-horizon state**: half risk when the NIFTY 500 24- or 36-month return is negative | 12/24/36 months | Different information from the 200-DMA; the published effect is about expected return, not timing |
| 4 | **Point-in-time breadth sizing**: half risk when fewer than X% of current members are above their 200-DMA *and* breadth is falling | X = 30/40/50%; 10-day slope | No external evidence, so this is pure hypothesis testing. Survivorship-safe only if built from the membership file. Watch for missed rebounds, since the literature calls low breadth contrarian |
| 5 | **Wide ratcheting chandelier alongside the Supertrend**: exit at the next open after the close falls below max(HH22 − k×ATR22, previous stop), whichever exit fires first | k = 3/4/5/6; also weekly bars | Tests whether giving back less helps without cutting the right tail. Tight settings are expected to fail; look for a plateau at k ≥ 4 |
| 6 | **Slow EMA exit replacing the Supertrend**: weekly close below the weekly EMA | EMA 10/21/30 weeks | Simplest slow trend exit. Directly comparable on the same entries |
| 7 | **Time-stop tuning**: `TIME_STOP_DAYS` × `TIME_STOP_R` | 15/25/40 days × 0/0.25/0.5R | Time exits rank best in the head-to-head evidence; check the current setting sits on a plateau rather than a spike |
| 8 | **India VIX as a sizing input only**: 0.75× risk when VIX is above its 1-year 80th percentile | 70/80/90th percentile | Low prior, since the evidence is contrarian. Data only from about 2008. Include it to rule it out, not to adopt it |

Do not run a give-back-of-profit trailing stop, a tight 2×ATR stop or a profit target. The evidence against them is consistent enough that testing them only adds chances to overfit. Variants 1 and 2 are cheap to implement as changes to `risk_mult`, which the backtest already records per entry, and they can be combined only after each passes on its own. If neither improves Calmar out of sample, the honest conclusion is that S44984096's drawdown since end-2024 is the normal cost of a momentum premium that has had no losing 5-year window in India, and the existing drawdown-halving rule is already the right defence.

## Conclusion

The literature changes the question. "Which filter would have kept me out of 2018 and 2025?" invites overfitting to two episodes, and Freefincal's Indian overlay shows a filter tuned this way can make things worse. The better question is whether risk should *scale* with how the strategy itself is doing and how the market is trending. That approach has cross-market support, generates few extra trades under Indian STT, and fits the drawdown-halving rule S44984096 already uses. On exits, the evidence says the current design (a conditional time stop plus a slow weekly trend exit) is closer to what survives than any tighter trailing stop. Any change should be a wider, ratcheting variant tested on a plateau, not a tighter one.

There are real gaps. No Indian study tests breadth or VIX filters out of sample. No study anywhere compares chandelier, ATR, EMA and time exits on the same entry with full costs. Several of the figures above come from vendors or blogs that cannot be rerun. This repo has point-in-time NIFTY 500 membership, a full Indian cost model and walk-forward capability, so it can produce better evidence than anything published on this question.
