# Lever a diversified book, not a new signal

No published, cost-inclusive, point-in-time Indian study shows any long-only stock-selection family reaching **16% CAGR with a max drawdown no worse than -30% over 2006-2026**. The target is reachable only when exposure is cut in bear regimes, and the families that cut it are the ones the repo already uses: a market trend gate, stock-level stops and volatility scaling. The families worth testing, in order: (1) volatility-scaled S44984096 used as the base and run at a higher risk budget; (2) a trend-gated, low-turnover momentum or 52-week-high sleeve with slow exits and a portfolio volatility target; (3) a trend-filtered price-only low-vol, or low-vol plus momentum, sleeve as the second system in a shared-risk book; (4) 52-week-high or Donchian breakouts with slow exits; (5) sector relative-strength used as a tilt on S44984096 entries rather than as a separate system. Short-term mean reversion (RSI(2), IBS) on NIFTY 500 stocks should be dropped. At about 1.06% per round trip, the cost drag alone runs into tens of percent a year. The route most likely to work does not add CAGR directly. It lowers the combined book's drawdown and time under water, then spends that headroom on risk per trade, for example 1% to 1.25%. **Treat 16% as a pre-tax target.** At 20% STCG on a book that realises most gains within a year, 16% pre-tax is about 12.8% after tax, and 16% after tax would need about 20% gross, which nothing in the evidence supports at -30% drawdown. This is research, not investment advice.

## Every fully invested factor book fell 45-75% in 2008

What the Indian evidence agrees on is simple. Stock selection alone does not survive 2008. A net-of-cost replica of Nifty 200 Momentum 30 (Dec 2006 to Jun 2025, annual rebalance, brokerage, slippage, 20% STCG / 12.5% LTCG) made **14.6% CAGR with a -70.6% max drawdown**, against 10.4% / -55.1% for NIFTY 50 ([freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-indian-momentum-investing-just-a-liquidity-illusion/)). Note that this is a platform vendor's backtest. The index itself fell **-67.9% in 2008, against -64.6% for NIFTY 50**. It fell -34.2% in March 2020 and about -27% in the 2024-25 unwind, when NIFTY 50 fell about -12% ([Curious Investing Insights](https://curiousinvestinginsights.substack.com/p/momentum-or-mayhem-unpacking-the)). Live tracking funds fell **-30.1% from 27 Sep 2024 to 14 Mar 2025** and were still below that peak in August 2026 ([arthgyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/fund/kotak-nifty-200-momentum-30-index-fund-direct-growth/151781)). The repo's own 12-1 rotation result (23% / -60 to -69%) matches this literature. It is not a bug.

The defensive factors cushion crashes but do not escape them. NIFTY100 Low Volatility 30 back-tests to **17.7% CAGR at 18.2% volatility from April 2005 to May 2018**, but its max drawdown was **-49.5% in 2008**, against -61.5% for NIFTY 100 ([NSE whitepaper](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY100_Low_Vol30_White_paper_Jun2018.pdf)). Alpha Low-Vol 30, the 50/50 price-only blend, back-tests to **20.3% CAGR but lost -50.3% in 2008** ([NSE Multi-Factor whitepaper](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_whitepaper.pdf)). Its live record is far weaker: about -12% over the year to September 2025 ([Kotak](https://www.kotaksecurities.com/indices/indian-indices/nifty-alpha-low-volatility-30)), and only 6.9% CAGR over the three years to 2020 ([NSE 2020 whitepaper](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty-alpha-low-volatility-30/nifty-alpha-low-volatility-30-whitepaper_2020.pdf)). This is the usual gap between back-calculated and live results. Predictor returns run **26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication** ([McLean and Pontiff](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)). Budget for that decay on any factor index result.

S44984096 reaches -23.5% because its regime gate and stops hold cash, not because of what it picks. That fixes the research agenda. Stock-selection variants (residual momentum, 52-week high, smoothness) can raise return per unit of exposure. The drawdown cap has to come from the exposure layer. Two cost facts constrain the selection layer. First, within the same 30 momentum names the low-turnover half netted **19.4% against 8.5%** for the high-turnover half ([freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-indian-momentum-investing-just-a-liquidity-illusion/)). Second, rebalance days for the momentum index are crowded, with about Rs 16k crore moving on one constituent flip ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/nifty200-momentum30-a-flip-of-index-switch-jolts-16k-cr-across-the-grid-125121400388_1.html)). Slow, buffered and off-calendar rebalancing is a robust choice. Exact lookbacks are fragile.

## Volatility scaling is the one overlay with crash evidence

Scaling momentum by its own realised volatility **raised the Sharpe ratio from 0.53 to 0.97** and "virtually eliminates crashes" in US long-short data ([EconPapers, Barroso and Santa-Clara](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:116:y:2015:i:1:p:111-120); [CFA Institute](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/blog/2018/01/17/timing-the-market-momentum-and-beyond)). Indian work points the same way. Risk-managed momentum on 450 BSE stocks about doubles the adjusted Sharpe ([FIIB Business Review 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/fbbsrw/v11y2022i3p347-358.html)), and India VIX-managed momentum extends the mean-variance frontier ([MSE WP 242](https://mse.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/WORKING-PAPER-242.pdf)). The skeptical result matters as much: generic volatility timing beat the unmanaged portfolio in only **53 of 103 equity portfolios out of sample** ([Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). The repo's own V1 result, a 2017-26 walk-forward Calmar of 0.70-0.74 against 0.63, is the right kind of evidence. It is modest, which fits a technique that helps at the margin rather than transforming results.

What volatility scaling buys is drawdown headroom. If V1 brings max drawdown to roughly -18 to -20% at similar CAGR, then raising risk per trade from 1% toward 1.25-1.4% moves CAGR toward 16-17% and keeps drawdown inside -30%. The sizing maths is linear in exposure, and costs scale with it. This is the shortest path to the target and the one the user should test first. It is also a leverage of an in-sample edge, so the doubled-slippage and holdout checks below are mandatory, not optional. Volatility scaling reacts late to V-shaped crashes such as March 2020 and lags the April-June 2020 rebound. It does nothing against the 2024-25 pattern, where index volatility stayed moderate while momentum names collapsed. Stock-level stops handle that pattern, and S44984096 already has them.

## A trend-filtered low-vol sleeve is the most plausible second system

Combining systems helps only as much as their correlation allows, and **blended CAGR lands between the sleeves; it does not add**. A trend sleeve with a -39.9% drawdown and a mean-reversion sleeve with -22.6% blended to -26.0%, with Sharpe 0.91 against 0.65 for each sleeve and return in between ([Price Action Lab](https://priceactionlab.com/Blog/?p=67828)). An Australian 50/50 blend cut drawdown from 15.3% to 10.5% while CAGR fell from 19.1% to 17.2% ([The Chartist](https://www.thechartist.com.au/?p=15968)). The large Sharpe gains in "Value and Momentum Everywhere" come from long-short sleeves with negative correlation ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/journal-article/value-and-momentum-everywhere)). Long-only Indian sleeves are nothing like that. NSE's dashboard shows Nifty200 Momentum 30 with **0.86 correlation** to its benchmark and Nifty100 Low Vol 30 with **0.96** ([NSE Index Dashboard Sep 2026](https://niftyindices.com/Index_Dashboard/Index_Dashboard_SEP2026.pdf)). The arithmetic follows. Two equal-volatility sleeves at correlation ρ give blend volatility σ·√((1+ρ)/2): a 3% reduction at ρ = 0.9 and 13% at ρ = 0.5. The gain comes from drawdowns arriving at different times, not from true decorrelation.

Low-vol is the best candidate for different timing. NSE describes it as defensive (FMCG, pharma, IT), while Alpha is pro-cyclical. In 2015-16 Alpha made +16.9% and +19.7% while Low-Vol made +11.7% and +1.8% ([NSE Multi-Factor whitepaper](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_whitepaper.pdf)). In 2011 Low-Vol lost -13.3% against -25.8% for NIFTY 100 ([NSE](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY100_Low_Vol30_White_paper_Jun2018.pdf)). It also has few Indian implementation traps: large caps, quarterly rebalancing, low turnover and more holdings past 12 months at 12.5% LTCG. Its weak spots are high-beta rallies (2017, 2023-24 PSU and smallcap runs) and its 2008 depth. That depth is why it needs the same NIFTY 500 200-DMA gate or an absolute-momentum exit to cash. Correlation with S44984096 is unmeasured. A guess of 0.5-0.7 on monthly returns is a hypothesis, and the repo can measure it cheaply. Mean reversion is the wrong second system for two reasons. It buys dips, as S44984096 already does, and its turnover is fatal at Indian costs (next section). Breakouts and 52-week-high systems buy strength, but they are trend-long books in the same universe and will probably correlate at 0.7 or more with S44984096.

Design the combination as two sleeves with equal risk (each scaled by its realised volatility) under one shared cap: total open risk no more than 6-8%, at most 2 names per sector, and the 10% drawdown halving rule applied to the combined equity curve. One shared 10-slot pool is simpler, but it lets the hotter system crowd the other out. Separate risk budgets keep the diversification.

## Mean reversion fails on cost arithmetic, rotation works only gated

No cost-inclusive, point-in-time Indian test of RSI(2), IBS or DMA pullbacks exists. The one large Indian RSI study used RSI 7/14/21, today's constituents and no costs ([PhonePe Invest](https://www.phonepeinvest.com/buzz/insights/the-rsi-myth-is-this-popular-indicator-worth-using-part-2-research-report)). Global evidence says simple short-term reversal is "hard or impossible to exploit" after costs, outside large caps ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-in-stocks)). Costs still took more than two-thirds of a composite short-term signal's return ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/alpha-from-short-term-signals/)). The Indian arithmetic settles it. At 1.06% per round trip, a 5-slot RSI(2) book with 4-5 day holds makes 150-250 round trips a year. That is a drag of roughly **30-50% of capital a year**, so each trade would need a gross edge of about 1.5-2% just to net 16% pre-tax, all taxed at 20% STCG. On NIFTY 500 delivery, drop it. The only version worth keeping is a slow pullback to the 50-DMA held for weeks (20-40 trades a year), and that is essentially S44984096 again.

Sector rotation has academic support in India. Part of stock momentum is a sector effect ([Sehgal and Jain 2011](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/09727981111129327/full/html)). As a standalone system, though, it inherits momentum's 2008 drawdown unless an absolute-momentum switch moves it to cash. Informal dual-momentum tests (NIFTY against gold) show about a -21% drawdown through 2008, compared with more than -50% for NIFTY ([Samasthiti](https://samasthiti.substack.com/p/indian-edition-of-the-dual-momentum)). One 2014-24 blog reports 18.8% / -11.4% ([MomentumLab](https://momentumlab.substack.com/p/backtesting-dual-momentum-strategy)). Both are short, informal and use gold, which is outside this universe. The better use of RS is as a filter on S44984096 entries: take signals only from the top-ranked third of sectors by 6-month return relative to NIFTY 50. That adds no new trades or costs, and it targets 2017-19, when large-cap private banks and IT held up while small and mid caps fell.

## Five families to test, with rules and grids

The ranking below weighs the chance of reaching 16% / -30% net of costs, the strength of the evidence and the diversification value. For every test, fit on 2006-2016 and hold out 2017-2026 without re-tuning. Rerun with slippage doubled to 0.2% per side. Rank results on max drawdown, longest time under water and Calmar before CAGR. Report the 2008, 2013, 2018-19, Feb-Jun 2020 and Sep 2024-Mar 2025 windows separately. Enter at the next open, skip fills when the bar is locked at its circuit band, add slippage on gap-ups above 3%, and require a 20-day median traded value of at least Rs 5-10 crore.

Dropped: RSI(2)/IBS/3-day-pullback mean reversion (cost arithmetic), ungated monthly rotation (2008 depth) and VCP patterns (contraction counts cannot be coded objectively, and the only "evidence" is hindsight-selected) ([FinancialWisdomTV](https://www.financialwisdomtv.com/post/can-one-chart-pattern-beat-the-market-i-tested-the-top-100-stocks)).

Some knobs are robust and some are fragile. The robust ones are the slow market gate, a 252-day vol lookback, buffered quarterly rebalancing, a liquidity floor and exits no tighter than 3 ATR. In US and Canadian trend-template tests, a 10% stop lowered CAGR without lowering drawdown, while a 200-DMA index filter cut drawdown by about 5 points ([MarketInOut TSX](https://www.marketinout.com/blog/minervini-trend-template-backtest-tsx)). Earlier work in this repo found the same: wide exits survive and tight trailing stops do not. The fragile knobs are exact momentum lookbacks, Donchian lengths of 20 days, single RSI thresholds and the rebalance date. Distrust any cell that beats its neighbours by more than a little. Breakouts have their own warning: a classic Donchian 20 on the NIFTY index had negative median P&L in every variant tested and spent about 5 years in drawdown ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/trend-following-in-india-part-1-testing-donchian-s-classic-rules/188234)). Only slow-lookback, slow-exit versions deserve a slot.

Indian traps to handle in code start with circuits. Non-F&O stocks have fixed bands up to 20%, and F&O names have dynamic 10% ranges ([Chittorgarh](https://www.chittorgarh.com/newportal/stock-nse-circuit-filters.asp)), so a breakout close or stop price on a locked day is not a real fill. ASM/GSM lists must be applied point-in-time, because using today's list is look-ahead, and buying is restricted from GSM stage 2 ([NSE GSM FAQ 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com//web/mediaattachment/2026-06/FAQs_-_Graded_Surveillance_Measure_GSM_Version_1.1_20260612125858.pdf)). Survivorship still matters at the smallcap end: survivor-only NIFTY Smallcap 250 backtests overstate returns by about **4.9 points a year** ([arXiv 2603.19380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19380)). Budget-day and results-season gaps sit beyond any ATR stop. Finally, check whether the 31 March 2026 STT circular changed delivery STT before freezing the cost model ([NSE circular](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/FATAX73524.pdf)).

## Conclusion

The 16% / -30% target is realistic only as a pre-tax, whole-book target reached through risk allocation. It is not realistic as a property of a new signal. Every Indian factor family arrives with 45-75% crash depth and a live record weaker than its backtest. The repo's edge is the exposure discipline it already has. The productive sequence follows from that: confirm V1 volatility scaling in the holdout, measure the real correlation of a gated low-vol sleeve with S44984096, and only then raise base risk to use whatever drawdown headroom the combined book has earned. If the combined holdout drawdown does not come in below about -20%, there is no headroom, and 15% is about the honest ceiling for this universe and cost structure. No family here fixes the 2017-19 and late-2024 lag. Those were rotation and midcap-bear periods. The realistic goal for them is less time under water, not outperformance, and a lower CAGR in those years is the cost of staying in the game.
