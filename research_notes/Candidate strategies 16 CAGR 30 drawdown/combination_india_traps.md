# Combining low-correlation equity strategies, and Indian-market traps for systematic long-only strategies

Scope note: I hit the tool budget with several primary NSE PDFs (factsheets, the momentum whitepaper, the multi-factor whitepaper, the Alpha Low-Vol whitepaper) timing out or being image-only, so most factor-index drawdown figures below come from secondary sources. Treat them as indicative. Everything not sourced is in Inferences or Gaps.

## Q1. How much does combining strategies or factors improve return-to-drawdown?

### Takeaway
Combining clearly improves Sharpe and drawdown when sleeve correlation is low or negative (value plus momentum is the textbook case). Blended CAGR lands *between* the sleeves; it does not add. Most published equity trend-plus-mean-reversion evidence is single backtests (in-sample). A sleeve with a negative or near-zero correlation to the current pullback system could plausibly cut max DD by a quarter to a third at similar CAGR, but it will not lift CAGR from 15.3% to above 16% unless the new sleeve's own CAGR is above 16% or leverage or a higher risk budget is used.

### Cited Findings
- Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen (J. Finance 2013) find value and momentum premia in eight markets or asset classes, and a negative correlation between value and momentum within and across asset classes. An equal-weight combination has a significantly higher Sharpe than either alone and is more stable across markets and periods — [AQR, Value and Momentum Everywhere](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/journal-article/value-and-momentum-everywhere); [paper PDF](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~lpederse/papers/ValMomEverywhere.pdf)
- AQR US single-market example: value Sharpe 0.63, momentum 0.29, 50/50 rank-based combination 1.23, with combination volatility about 5% (long-short, not long-only) — [AQR, Intuition Underlying Multi-Factor Stock Selection](https://www.aqr.com/Research-Archive/Perspectives/Looking-for-the-Intuition-Underlying-Multi-Factor-Stock-Selection)
- Price Action Lab ensemble backtest (in-sample, not equity-only): trend sleeve max DD -39.9%, mean-reversion sleeve -22.6%, equal-weight blend -26.0%. Blend Sharpe 0.91 against 0.65 for each sleeve alone. The blend's annualized return (11.3%) lies between the components — [Price Action Lab](https://priceactionlab.com/Blog/?p=67828)
- The Chartist (Australian equities, practitioner backtest): a 50/50 blend of mean reversion and trend cut max DD from about 15.3% to 10.5%, while CAGR fell from 19.1% to 17.2% — [The Chartist](https://www.thechartist.com.au/?p=15968)
- Textbook test (MACD trend plus RSI mean reversion): low correlation between the systems smoothed combined equity drawdowns — [Mechanical Trading Systems, ch. 9 (Wiley/O'Reilly)](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/mechanical-trading-systems/9780471730972/chapter09-sec003.html)
- Sentiment signals buffered trend-following in its weakest periods but dragged in strong uptrends — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/trend-following-and-relative-sentiment/)
- Trend overlays materially reduced drawdowns in a 60/40 portfolio, 1960–2017 — [Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/strategic-rebalancing-summary)
- NSE's own multi-factor indices use fixed equal factor weights. For example, Alpha Quality Low-Vol 30 is 33.33% each, and Alpha Low-Vol 30 is 50/50, drawn from Nifty 100 plus Midcap 50 — [NSE Multi-Factor whitepaper](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_Multi-Factor_Indices_whitepaper.pdf); [Alpha Quality Low-Vol 30 factsheet](https://www.niftyindices.com/Factsheet/Factsheet_NIFTY_Alpha_Quality_Low-Volatility_30.pdf)
- NSE Sept 2026 dashboard: Nifty200 Momentum 30 has 1-year volatility of 18.63% and correlation 0.86 to its benchmark. Nifty100 Low Volatility 30 has 12.18% and 0.96. These are correlations to the market, not to each other — [NSE Index Dashboard Sep 2026](https://niftyindices.com/Index_Dashboard/Index_Dashboard_SEP2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- Long-only sleeves inside one equity market share beta. Typical long-only factor-sleeve correlations are 0.8–0.95, not the negative values in long-short papers. The diversification gain is mostly in the *timing* of drawdowns (low-vol falls less in crashes, momentum crashes at reversals), not in true decorrelation. The dashboard market correlations of 0.86 and 0.96 support this.
- Simple arithmetic: two sleeves with equal vol σ and correlation ρ give blend vol σ·√((1+ρ)/2). At ρ=0.9 that is about 0.97σ (3% reduction), at ρ=0.5 about 0.87σ, and at ρ=0 about 0.71σ. Long-only equity sleeves sit near ρ 0.7–0.9, so expect perhaps a 5–15% vol reduction and a similar or slightly larger drawdown reduction, because drawdowns are timing-dependent.
- For S44984096 (15.3% / -23.5%), the realistic gain from a second long-only sleeve is a shallower DD (perhaps -18 to -21%) at blended CAGR, not a higher CAGR. A combined DD below -23.5% would leave room to raise risk per trade (for example 1% to 1.25%) and lift CAGR within the -30% limit. That "lever the diversified book" route is the most credible path to 16%+, and it is how AQR-style combinations are used in practice.
- Shared-slot design (one 10-slot book, signals from both systems competing) automatically caps total open risk, but lets the hotter system crowd out the other. Separate capital (for example 50/50, rebalanced monthly or quarterly) preserves diversification but doubles the position count or halves position size; at 10 slots of 1% risk each, splitting to 5+5 raises single-name concentration noise. Equal-risk allocation (scale each sleeve by its realized vol or DD) beats equal-capital when sleeve vols differ, as with momentum at 18.6% and low-vol at 12.2%.
- Mean reversion on Indian stocks (buying short-term oversold stocks in uptrends) is likely *highly* correlated with an existing pullback-in-uptrend system, since both buy dips. A genuinely different sleeve would be cross-sectional momentum or rotation (buy strength) or low-vol/quality (defensive).

### Gaps
- No primary source found for measured return correlations between Indian trend, momentum, mean-reversion and low-vol long-only sleeves. This needs an in-repo test: correlate monthly returns of S44984096 with Nifty200 Momentum 30 and Nifty100 Low Vol 30 TRI.
- No large out-of-sample study found for equity trend plus mean reversion. The cited blends are single in-sample backtests.
- The global "Value and Momentum Everywhere" combined Sharpe figure was not extracted.

## Q2. Indian smart-beta and factor-index anchors, 2005/06–2026

### Takeaway
Indian factor indices delivered high-teens CAGR over long backtests, but those are mostly back-calculated (in-sample) before their launch dates. Momentum's max DD (about -68% in 2008) was *worse* than NIFTY 50. Low-vol and multi-factor indices did better in drawdowns, but I could not verify their exact max DDs. A long-only, fully-invested factor index does not meet a -30% DD limit through 2008. Only a timing or cash-raising overlay, like the current strategy's trend filter, gets there.

### Cited Findings
- Nifty Alpha Low-Volatility 30: 19.0% CAGR since 2005 against 13.2% for Nifty 100 and 9.9% for Nifty Midcap 50 (data to about Sept 2020; mostly back-tested, since the index launched later); last 3 years 6.9% CAGR — [NSE Alpha Low-Vol 30 whitepaper 2020](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty-alpha-low-volatility-30/nifty-alpha-low-volatility-30-whitepaper_2020.pdf)
- Nifty200 Momentum 30 drawdowns from ATH: 2008 GFC -67.9% (Nifty 50 -64.6%), COVID 2020 -34.2% (-39.6%), 2022–23 -14.8% (-13.2%), and the slump to Mar 2025 -27% (-12%). It beat Nifty 50 in 14 of 20 calendar years — [Curious Investing Insights](https://curiousinvestinginsights.substack.com/p/momentum-or-mayhem-unpacking-the) (secondary; values read from charts)
- Momentum 30 construction: top 30 of Nifty 200 by normalized 6- and 12-month momentum, weighted by volatility, rebalanced semi-annually. It suffers more in bearish phases — [freefincal](https://freefincal.com/nifty200-momentum-30-index-a-new-strategy-index-from-the-nse/)
- Sharpely shows Nifty 200 Momentum 30 3-year CAGR 8.09% and 5-year 5.84% (undated, unverified) — [Sharpely](https://sharpely.in/indices/nifty-200-momentum-30/58/index-technicals)
- Oct 2026 NSE dashboard row for Momentum 30 (columns unlabeled in extract): "-3.12 -6.41 -8.48 9.41 7.93 19.42 1.15 0.82 0.68" — [NSE Index Dashboard Oct 2026](https://niftyindices.com/Index_Dashboard/Index_Dashboard_OCT2026.pdf). Probably 1M/3M/6M/1Y returns, then returns since inception, volatility, beta and correlation, but this is unconfirmed.

### Inferences
- A rough anchor: the best Indian long-only factor indices made about 15–20% CAGR from 2005 to 2020 in back-tests, decaying to single digits in 2024–26 (momentum's 5-year 5.84%, if that figure is right). Live (post-launch) results are clearly weaker than the back-calculated history. That is the classic in-sample-versus-live gap.
- Fully-invested factor indices had 2008 DDs around -55 to -68%. The current strategy's -23.5% max DD comes from its trend and regime filter holding cash, not from stock selection. A 16% CAGR at -30% DD over 2006–2026 is therefore above what passive factor indices offered on a DD-adjusted basis. It is plausible only with an active exposure filter, and it is at the upper edge of what is realistic net of costs and tax.
- Index figures are gross of costs and tax (TRI). Monthly-rotation retail replication would lose another 1–3%/yr (see Q3).

### Gaps

The PDFs timed out, or the multi-factor whitepaper is image-only. Get the TRI series from niftyindices.com historical data and compute 2006–2026 CAGR and max DD in-repo.
- Base and launch dates for each index (needed to split back-test from live) were not verified.

## Q3. Indian execution and data traps

### Takeaway
The biggest quantifiable traps are survivorship (about 5 pp/yr in small caps), STT and tax drag that scales with turnover, and fills through circuits, gaps and surveillance stages. With NIFTY 500 point-in-time data, a 20% band cap and an ASM/GSM exclusion, most of these are controllable. Fills at the open on result-day gaps remain the main residual risk.

### Cited Findings
- **Price bands:** circuit filters come in tiers of 2%, 5%, 10%, 20% and none, set daily from the prior close. Non-F&O stocks have fixed bands capped at 20%. F&O stocks have no fixed band, but a 10% dynamic operating range that is relaxed in 5% steps — [Chittorgarh](https://www.chittorgarh.com/newportal/stock-nse-circuit-filters.asp); [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/news/sebi-to-enforce-dynamic-price-bands-for-all-fo-stocks-equally-on-exchanges); [NSE sliding price band FAQ](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQ_on_Sliding_Price_Bands.pdf)
- NSE revises bands frequently. In Dec 2025 it revised 230 stocks, with 128 raised from 5% to 20% and 90 moved to 10% — [Kotak Securities](https://www.kotaksecurities.com/news/market-news/nse-price-band-revision-stock-volatility-update)
- **ASM/GSM:** these measures include price-band reduction, periodic call auction and move to the trade-for-trade segment. ASM criteria cover high-low variation, client concentration, band hits, close-to-close variation and PE. From the 2018 framework: a 5% band and 100% margin on inclusion. GSM was cut from 6 to 4 stages (2026 FAQ), and buying is restricted from GSM stage 2 and above. The long-term ASM exit requires 90 calendar days in the framework, subject to stage rules — [NSE ASM FAQ Aug 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com//web/mediaattachment/2026-08/FAQs_-_Additional_Surveillance_Measure_ASM_20260812103311.pdf); [NSE GSM FAQ 2026](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com//web/mediaattachment/2026-06/FAQs_-_Graded_Surveillance_Measure_GSM_Version_1.1_20260612125858.pdf); [Business Standard 2018](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/bse-nse-create-framework-to-bring-stocks-under-enhanced-surveillance-118062500692_1.html); [Zerodha](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/trading-faqs/articles/what-does-gsm-mean)
- **Survivorship:** a 2026 preprint (not peer-reviewed) on the NIFTY Smallcap 250 using bhavcopy data including delisted stocks finds survivor-only backtests overstate returns by about 4.9 pp/yr (26.17% vs 21.23%). Its constituent reconstruction is about 85–90% accurate — [arXiv 2603.19380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19380). An earlier finding (Agarwalla, Jacob and Varma 2013, as reported secondhand) is that the bias was negligible for BSE constituents — [rkohli3 write-up](https://rkohli3.github.io/india-famafrench/Fama.html) (conflicting; secondary)
- Backtests that exclude delisted stocks are biased upward, especially for weak or 52-week-low names — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2016/08/dealing-with-delistings-a-critical-aspect-for-stock-selection-research/)
- **Costs (delivery, 2026):** STT 0.1% on buy and on sell. Stamp duty 0.015% on buy only. NSE transaction charge about 0.00297–0.00325% (sources differ). 18% GST on brokerage and exchange or SEBI fees, not on STT or stamp. Discount-broker delivery brokerage is often ₹0 — [Groww](https://groww.in/blog/what-is-stt); [Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/securities-transaction-tax); [Sharpely calculator](https://sharpely.in/calculators/brokerage-calculator); [NSE levies page](https://www.nseindia.com/static/invest/first-time-investor-sebi-turnover-fees-stt-other-levies). An NSE circular dated 31 Mar 2026 on STT changes exists, but its content was not verified — [NSE circular FATAX73524](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/FATAX73524.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Cost drag by turnover:** statutory costs are about 0.22–0.25% round trip. With the repo's 0.86% round trip plus 0.1% slippage per side, a round trip costs about 1.06%. At 100% annual turnover the drag is about 1.1%/yr, at 300% about 3.2%/yr, at 600% about 6.4%/yr. Any added mean-reversion sleeve with weekly holds could cost more in costs than it adds in diversification.
- **Tax drag:** at 20% STCG (holds under 12 months), a 16% pre-tax swing system nets about 12.8% if all gains are realized each year. A low-turnover momentum or low-vol sleeve held over 12 months pays 12.5% LTCG (with the ₹1.25 lakh exemption). Judge the 16% target pre-tax, explicitly, or the bar becomes about 20% gross.
- **Circuits:** a backtest that fills a stop at the close or at the stop price on a lower-circuit day is unrealistic. With a 20% band, a stock can lock limit-down for several sessions (seen in small caps and ASM names). Model it as no fill while the close is at the lower band, then fill at the next open.
- **Results gaps:** quarterly results (mid-Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct) cause overnight gaps far beyond ATR stops. Monthly-rebalance entries in the first week of a results month carry the most gap risk. Test a "no entry within N days of a results date" rule. This needs a results-calendar dataset (BSE corporate announcements).
- **ASM/GSM survivorship twist:** historical ASM/GSM lists are needed point-in-time. Excluding stocks using *today's* list is look-ahead.
- **T+1:** settlement is T+1 (fully since Jan 2023), so sale proceeds are available the next day. Same-day sell-then-buy rotation works within broker credit, but the BTST/short-delivery risk is real for names hitting circuits.
- **NIFTY 500 point-in-time:** this removes most survivorship from delistings but not from index-reconstitution timing. NSE rebalances semi-annually, with the announcement about 4 weeks ahead, so membership should be applied from the effective date.
- **Small-cap impact:** 0.1% slippage is reasonable for NIFTY 100 names but optimistic for the bottom of the NIFTY 500 (Smallcap 250) at larger capital sizes. A liquidity floor (for example a 20-day median traded value at least 50–100× position size) would be a sensible filter.

- No quantified study of impact cost by NIFTY 500 market-cap bucket was found (NSE publishes impact cost per stock, but it was not retrieved).
- The content of the 31 Mar 2026 STT circular was not confirmed. Check whether delivery STT changed in Budget 2026.
- No reliable source quantifying survivorship bias for the full NIFTY 500 (only the Smallcap 250 preprint).
